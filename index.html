ACT ONE

What is true? (01–02)

The raw feed arrives with duplicates, gaps, and identities that don't reconcile. Nothing downstream is trustworthy until it survives inspection — and until someone has looked and found where the margin actually lives.

01
Data
STEP 1 OF 8

Data validation

Nothing downstream is trustworthy until the raw feed is. This stage is the bouncer at the door.

Input
Raw transactions, market notes, schemas, business rules
Activity
Checks required fields, missing values, duplicates, ranges, types and cross-field consistency
Product
Validated datasets, quality summary, validation report
Why this stage exists
Every later estimate inherits these rows. Defects here do not raise errors, they propagate quietly.
If this is wrong
Duplicate rows double demand and elasticity absorbs it without complaint.
Technology
pandas, NumPy, custom rule checks
Alternatives
Pandera, Great Expectations, dbt tests
What the business sees
A data-quality scorecard: how many rows passed, what was dropped and why
Product feeds Step 02 · Exploratory analysis
02
Data
STEP 2 OF 8

Exploratory analysis

Before modelling anything, you look. Where is the money, where is the discounting, where is the drift?

Input
Validated retail data
Activity
Computes commercial KPIs by product, category, store, region, promotion and time
Product
KPI tables, portfolio summaries, trend and discount analysis
Why this stage exists
Direction before modelling. Knowing where margin lives decides what is worth optimizing at all.
If this is wrong
You model the whole catalogue evenly and miss that a handful of items drive the result.
Technology
pandas, Plotly
Alternatives
SQL data marts, Power BI, Tableau
What the business sees
Portfolio dashboards: top and bottom performers, margin leaks, discount depth by category
Product feeds Step 03 · Price elasticity
ACT TWO

What will happen? (03–06)

How much demand moves when price moves. Whether that's causal or just an artifact of cutting prices on weak weeks. What promotions genuinely add. And what next period looks like if nothing changes at all.

03
Models
STEP 3 OF 8

Price elasticity

The core question: if I move price by one percent, how much demand do I lose?

Input
Historical price and demand history
Activity
Estimates the demand response to a price change, per item
Product
Elasticity estimates, model statistics, demand-sensitivity classes
Why this stage exists
It is the first number a pricing team asks for and the coefficient step 08 leans on hardest.
If this is wrong
Clipping discount outliers attenuates elasticity toward zero and invites false confidence.
Technology
statsmodels, log-log regression
Alternatives
Bayesian regression, hierarchical models, GAMs
What the business sees
Each item tagged elastic, inelastic or roughly unit elastic, with a confidence on that tag
Product feeds Step 04 · Causal price analysis
04
Models
STEP 4 OF 8

Causal price analysis

Correlation lies. Prices get cut because demand was already weak, so a naive elasticity is biased.

Input
Demand, price, controls, instruments such as cost shocks
Activity
Tests whether the observed demand change was actually caused by price
Product
OLS versus IV comparison, causal estimates, first-stage diagnostics
Why this stage exists
Price reacts to demand as much as demand reacts to price. An instrument breaks that loop.
If this is wrong
A weak instrument passes quietly and every downstream recommendation inherits the bias.
Technology
linearmodels (2SLS), statsmodels
Alternatives
Experiments, difference-in-differences, synthetic control
What the business sees
A credibility check: how far the naive number sits from the causal one
Product feeds Step 05 · Promotion uplift
05
Models
STEP 5 OF 8

Promotion uplift

Some promotions create demand. Most discount customers who would have bought anyway.

Input
Promotion flags, demand, price, product, store, historical features
Activity
Estimates the incremental demand a promotion actually generates
Product
Uplift predictions, ranked opportunities, segment summaries, saved model
Why this stage exists
Promotion spend is only defensible where uplift is incremental rather than coincident.
If this is wrong
A promo window misaligned by one day leaks promoted days into control and inflates uplift.
Technology
scikit-learn, XGBoost
Alternatives
Causal forests, X-learner, doubly robust models
What the business sees
A ranked list: which items are worth promoting, which promotions are burning margin
Product feeds Step 06 · Demand forecasting
06
Models
STEP 6 OF 8

Demand forecasting

Without a forecast of what happens if you do nothing, there is nothing to price a change against.

Input
Demand history, prices, promotions, calendar, lags, rolling features
Activity
Predicts future demand using chronological train, validation and test splits
Product
Item-level forecasts, evaluation metrics, feature importance, saved model
Why this stage exists
Optimization scores every candidate price against a do-nothing baseline. This is that baseline.
If this is wrong
Gaps in the calendar let lag and rolling features silently span missing time.
Technology
XGBoost, scikit-learn
Alternatives
LightGBM, CatBoost, ARIMA, Prophet, deep learning
What the business sees
Expected units per item next period, with accuracy stated plainly (MAE, MAPE)
Product feeds Step 07 · Market intelligence
ACT THREE

What should we do? (07–08)

The market enters as structured signal rather than prose. Every candidate price is scored against demand, margin and the rules the business won't break. One price is chosen — and it arrives carrying the constraint that bound it.

07
Decision
STEP 7 OF 8

Market intelligence

Competitor notes and sentiment live as free text. The optimizer can only read columns.

Input
Market notes, competitor observations, customer signals, trends
Activity
Converts unstructured text into structured pricing features
Product
Sentiment, competitor, price-sensitivity, promotion-interest and trend features
Why this stage exists
A price is set against a market, not just a demand curve. This is where the market enters.
If this is wrong
Recommendations arrive without context and get overridden on instinct by the category manager.
Technology
Retrieval over market notes, feature extraction
Alternatives
Embeddings, vector databases, LLM extraction, topic models
What the business sees
Context beside every recommendation: competitor undercutting, sentiment negative on quality
Product feeds Step 08 · Price optimization
08
Decision
STEP 8 OF 8

Price optimization

Everything above converges here. Feasible prices are scored against demand, margin and the rules the business won't break.

Input
Price, cost, elasticity, forecasts, uplift, inventory, market features, constraints
Activity
Scores feasible price scenarios and selects the most suitable one
Product
Recommended prices with expected demand, revenue, profit, action type, constraint status
Why this stage exists
This is the only stage that decides. Everything before it is evidence.
If this is wrong
A stale cost or a missing constraint yields a recommendation finance or legal will reject.
Technology
SciPy, NumPy grid and constrained search
Alternatives
Nonlinear optimization, Bayesian optimization, reinforcement learning
What the business sees
The decision: raise, hold or cut. The new price, the expected profit delta, the binding guardrail
Final stage · Output is the pricing recommendation
!

This is not an automated pricing system. Nothing here writes a price to a till. Every recommendation is a proposal, and requires financial, legal, operational and governance review before it reaches a shelf.
